| F1 | Gloria Mark's computer-logging research: average time on one screen before switching fell to about 47 seconds; returning to a task after an interruption can take about 25 minutes (her book says 25; the script says "more than twenty"). This is not a general "attention span". | Gloria Mark, *Attention Span* (Hanover Square Press, 2023): <https://gloriamark.com/attention-span/> · UCI coverage: <https://ics.uci.edu/2023/01/12/cnn-your-attention-span-is-shrinking-studies-say-heres-how-to-stay-focused/> |
| F2 | Randomized, waitlist-controlled trial in *PNAS Nexus* (2025): 467 iPhone users (mostly US and Canada, recruited via Prolific); the Freedom app blocked all mobile internet (data and Wi-Fi) for 2 weeks; calls, texts and internet on other devices stayed available; one group blocked first, the other two weeks later; outcomes: sustained attention, mental health, subjective well-being. | Castelo, Kushlev, Ward, Esterman & Reiner (2025), "Blocking mobile internet on smartphones improves sustained attention, mental health, and subjective well-being", *PNAS Nexus* 4(2), pgaf017, doi:10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf017: <https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017> |
| F3 | Compliance: 119 of 467 committed participants (25.5%) were classed as compliant (block active at least 10 of 14 days). | Same paper (F2), Results. |
| F4 | Intervention group: average daily phone use fell from 314 to 161 minutes during the block, and rose to 265 minutes after the block ended. | Same paper (F2), Screen Time data. |
| F5 | Sustained attention (gradCPT, city/mountain images) improved; authors compare the effect to "about the same magnitude as 10 years of age-related decline"; the delayed group also improved after its block; mental health and subjective well-being improved; more time offline (in-person socializing, exercise, nature, hobbies), calls and texts unchanged; sleep, social connectedness and self-control improved; 91% improved on at least one of the three outcomes. | Same paper (F2) · Press summary: <https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250219/Blocking-mobile-internet-for-two-weeks-improves-mental-health-and-well-being.aspx> |
| F6 | Limitations stated by the authors: expectancy (placebo and demand) effects may have contributed; sample motivated to reduce phone use; completers had better baseline attention and mental health; generalizability may be limited; the tested mediators (such as time offline, sleep or social connection) did not explain the attention effect. | Same paper (F2), Discussion. |
| F7 | METR randomized trial (2025): 16 experienced developers, 246 tasks; with early-2025 AI tools tasks took 19% longer, while developers estimated afterwards that AI had made them 20% faster. Results are specific to that setting. | METR (2025), arXiv 2507.09089: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089> · <https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/11/ai_code_tools_slow_down/> |
